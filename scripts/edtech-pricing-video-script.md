# Video script: Should an EdTech Platform Raise Its Prices?

**Source:** Toledah case study, "Should an EdTech Platform Raise Its Prices?" (Sept 11, 2026)
**Length:** about 7 minutes
**Note:** the case uses constructed, illustrative data. The script says so on screen and in the voiceover.

---

## HOOK (0:00-0:30)

**[ON SCREEN: "₹1,499 → ₹2,599?" with a red X over it]**

**VO:** An EdTech company's pricing model said it should charge 73% more. The model was precise. It was also wrong to trust. Here's the number the team *did* choose instead, and why it lifts monthly contribution by 36.7% while losing customers.

**[ON SCREEN: "+36.7% contribution. -7.9% subscribers."]**

---

## SECTION 1: The setup (0:30-1:30)

**[VISUAL: single price card, "₹1,499 / month", with four icons stacked inside: recorded courses, tests, live classes, mentor support]**

**VO:** An Indian EdTech platform sells one plan: ₹1,499 a month. Recorded courses, tests, live classes and mentor support, all in one bundle.

**[CHART: table builds line by line]**

| Metric | Current |
|---|---|
| Subscribers | 8,000 |
| Monthly revenue | ₹11,992,000 |
| Variable cost / subscriber | ₹410 |
| Monthly contribution | ₹8,712,000 |
| Contribution margin | 72.6% |
| Monthly churn | 5.8% |

**VO:** The business is healthy. But management asks: are we charging the right price for what we provide?

**[ON SCREEN: "Contribution = (Price − Variable Cost) × Subscribers"]**

---

## SECTION 2: The real problem (1:30-2:30)

**[VISUAL: pie chart, 65% students / 35% working professionals]**

**VO:** The customer base is 65% students and 35% working professionals. Professionals are far less price-sensitive. Yet both groups pay the same ₹1,499 for the same bundle.

**[VISUAL: competitor ladder: EduMax ₹999 recorded courses / SkillPro ₹1,499 courses + tests / CareerEdge ₹2,299 courses + mentor support]**

**VO:** And look at the market. The platform is priced like SkillPro but delivers something closer to CareerEdge.

**[ON SCREEN: "The problem isn't underpricing. It's price-to-value segmentation."]**

---

## SECTION 3: How customers respond (2:30-3:30)

**[CHART: demand curve, new subscribers per month vs. price, from ₹1,199 (2,350) down to ₹1,899 (1,250). Second line: churn rising from 4.7% to 8.3%.]**

**VO:** Historical data shows the pattern. At ₹1,199 the platform adds 2,350 subscribers a month, with 4.7% churn. At ₹1,899 that falls to 1,250, and churn climbs to 8.3%.

**VO:** A single-plan analysis seems to say: keep raising the price, because revenue keeps going up. But hold on.

---

## SECTION 4: Why the "obvious" answer fails (3:30-4:45)

**[VISUAL: revenue curve climbing forever, with the label "No finite optimum"]**

**VO:** For professionals, the model assumes an elasticity of −0.5. With a constant-elasticity demand curve, that means revenue never peaks. The math says "charge more, forever." That's a property of the assumption, not evidence that customers will pay unlimited prices.

**[CHART: price axis, 1,499 → 2,599 highlighted, labelled "+73%"]**

**VO:** The model's top pick for the premium tier was ₹2,599, worth about ₹13.36 million a month in contribution. The team rejected it. The elasticity estimates came from small price moves, and ₹2,599 is a 73% jump far outside the data.

**[ON SCREEN: "A model can give a precise number and still be wrong about the world."]**

---

## SECTION 5: The better question (4:45-5:45)

**[VISUAL: single plan splits into three tiers]**

**VO:** So the team changed the question. Not "how high can we go?" but "what should we charge whom, and for what?"

**[CHART: tier table]**

| Tier | Price | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | ₹1,199 | Recorded courses + tests |
| Plus | ₹1,599 | Basic + live classes |
| Pro | ₹2,199 | Plus + mentor / career support |

**VO:** The optimization had guardrails. Every tier keeps at least a 65% contribution margin. Each tier is at least 25% above the one below. Basic stays under ₹1,199 for students. Existing customers face no more than a 10% effective increase. And at least 20% of professionals get a path to Pro.

---

## SECTION 6: The result (5:45-6:30)

**[CHART: side-by-side bars, current vs. recommended]**

| Metric | Current | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Subscribers | 8,000 | 7,367 |
| Monthly revenue | ₹11,992,000 | ₹14,929,009 |
| Monthly contribution | ₹8,712,000 | ₹11,908,443 |
| Contribution margin | 72.6% | 79.8% |

**VO:** Contribution rises 36.7%, from ₹8.71 million to ₹11.91 million. Subscribers drop 7.9%. The company gives up some volume for much stronger unit economics.

**[ON SCREEN: "The plan: pilot it. Don't bet the company on it."]**

**VO:** And it launches as a pilot, tracking tier adoption, conversion, churn, upgrades, downgrades and contribution per customer. Real behaviour beats extrapolation.

---

## SECTION 7: Takeaways and CTA (6:30-7:15)

**[ON SCREEN: numbered list, one line per beat]**

1. Revenue isn't value. Look at contribution.
2. Segments have different willingness to pay.
3. Pricing structure can matter more than the headline price.
4. Optimization informs judgment. It doesn't replace it.
5. Be skeptical of any model far outside its data.
6. Treat pricing as an experiment.

**VO:** Would you have gone for ₹2,599, or piloted the tiers? Tell me in the comments. If you want more case breakdowns like this, subscribe. Next up: how to estimate price elasticity from your own data.

**[END SCREEN: subscribe button + next video]**
